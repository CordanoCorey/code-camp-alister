# Royal Rangers program content and rights research

**Research date:** 2026-08-12

**Scope:** Royal Rangers USA program content, age groups, advancement and merits, Trail of the Saber, Gold Medal of Achievement, uniforms, ideals, Frontiersmen Camping Fellowship (FCF), handbook distribution, and the published copyright/trademark/licensing rules relevant to a third-party website or app.

**Source policy:** Primary, first-party sources only: Royal Rangers USA, the General Council of the Assemblies of God (GCAG), and Gospel Publishing House (GPH; formerly branded online as My Healthy Church).

This is a source review, not legal advice. “Not authorized by the published terms” means that the sources reviewed do not contain the needed permission; it is not a legal opinion about every possible use.

**Public-content boundary:** The program summaries below intentionally stop at public metadata and original high-level descriptions. They do not reproduce advancement or award requirements, lesson text, worksheets, handbook text, protected wording or artwork, or downloadable files. The authoritative pages remain linked at their publisher-controlled locations.

## Executive findings

1. Royal Rangers USA officially describes itself as an activity-based, small-group church ministry for boys and young men in grades K–12. A local church program is an **outpost**, which may contain one or more of four groups: Ranger Kids, Discovery Rangers, Adventure Rangers, and Expedition Rangers. ([Royal Rangers USA, “About Us”](https://royalrangers.com/about))
2. The official award name is **Trail of the Saber**, not “Path to the Saber.” It recognizes sustained participation in Royal Rangers junior leadership development; the national page remains the appropriate source for current details. ([Royal Rangers USA, “Trail of the Saber”](https://royalrangers.com/training/junior/tots))
3. The official site makes skill-merit requirement worksheets publicly reachable, but places leaders’ teaching materials, Bible lessons, leadership lessons, and award artwork in the paid TRaCclub delivery system. Public reachability is not an express republication license. ([Royal Rangers USA, “Merits Homepage”](https://royalrangers.com/merits); [Royal Rangers USA, “TRaCclub”](https://royalrangers.com/tracclub))
4. Royal Rangers handbooks are commercial publications. The four boys’ handbook product pages currently advertise print and e-book editions; the current Leader Manual listing is loose-leaf print, and the current FCF Handbook listing is paperback. The official curriculum page does not offer free, redistributable handbook files. ([Royal Rangers USA, “Curriculum”](https://royalrangers.com/curriculum); [GPH, “Royal Rangers Leader Manual”](https://myhealthychurch.com/store/startitem.cfm?item=022171); [GPH, “FCF Handbook”](https://myhealthychurch.com/store/startitem.cfm?item=020540))
5. GCAG/GPH policies do **not** grant a third-party site a blanket right to host full handbooks, mirror official pages, scrape or re-publish merit worksheets, distribute TRaCclub materials, or reuse official artwork. GPH says it administers reproduction permission for publications in print and electronic media and that uses outside its narrow published allowances require a written or electronic request. ([GPH, “Rights & Permissions”](https://myhealthychurch.com/store/startcat.cfm?cat=tRIGHTPER); [GCAG, “Terms of Use”](https://ag.org/Terms%20of%20Use); [GPH, “Terms of Use”](https://myhealthychurch.com/store/startcat.cfm?cat=tLEGAL))
6. The Royal Rangers name, group names, logos, emblems, and artwork are protected properties with a specific acceptable-use policy. A general worldwide directory/app is not expressly identified in that policy as an allowed use, so its brand use and exact notice requirements are unresolved until approved in writing. ([Royal Rangers USA, “Emblems & Trademarks Use”](https://royalrangers.com/policies/trademarks))

## 1. Program identity and age groups

Royal Rangers USA states that its mission is to give churches a platform to evangelize boys for Christ, equip them to become Christlike men, and empower them for lifelong servant leadership. It names seven program methods: friendship, activities, identity, achievement, discipleship, leadership, and service. ([Royal Rangers USA, “About Us”](https://royalrangers.com/about))

| Group | Official grade band | Advancement shape | Highest age-group recognition described by the official site |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Ranger Kids | K–2 | Fifteen steps split evenly across the Trail to the Elk, Trail to the Wolverine, and Trail to the Cougar; Ranger Kids does not use the same merit system as the older groups | Gold Trail Award ([Ranger Kids Advancement Trail](https://royalrangers.com/advancement/rangerkids/)) |
| Discovery Rangers | 3–5 | Bible, skill, and leadership merits; blue skill merits are the primary skill level | Gold Eagle Award ([Discovery Rangers Advancement Trail](https://royalrangers.com/advancement/discoveryrangers)) |
| Adventure Rangers | 6–8 | Bible, skill, and leadership merits; green skill merits are the primary skill level | Adventure Gold Award ([Adventure Rangers Advancement Trail](https://royalrangers.com/advancement/adventurerangers)) |
| Expedition Rangers | 9–12 | Skill and leadership merits plus Spirit Challenges; silver skill merits are the primary skill level | E3 Award ([Expedition Rangers Advancement Trail](https://royalrangers.com/advancement/expeditionrangers)) |

The national advancement page says the system has one trail per age group; merits form the core for Discovery, Adventure, and Expedition Rangers, while Ranger Kids follows a different process. ([Royal Rangers USA, “Advancement System”](https://royalrangers.com/advancement))

The Ranger Kids trail is designed as a three-year sequence: three named sub-trails contain five steps each, and the official page describes quarterly progress with one annual recognition per year. This is enough public structure for a navigation or overview page; the step requirements and checklists remain in official curriculum and the separately sold handbook. ([Royal Rangers USA, “Ranger Kids Advancement Trail”](https://royalrangers.com/advancement/rangerkids/); [GPH, “Ranger Kids Handbook”](https://myhealthychurch.com/store/startitem.cfm?item=022115))

### Public indexes versus paid teaching content

The official distinction is important:

- The national **Merits Homepage** links to all skill-merit requirements. ([Royal Rangers USA, “Merits Homepage”](https://royalrangers.com/merits))
- **TRaCclub** is an annual paid membership organized into Ranger Kids, Discovery, Adventure, Expedition, and Leaders Resource tracks. Its content includes weekly meeting guides, merit activity guides, handouts, Bible lessons, leadership merits, award artwork, recordkeeping materials, and devotions. ([Royal Rangers USA, “TRaCclub”](https://royalrangers.com/tracclub))
- The boys’ handbooks and Leader Manual are explicitly **not** part of TRaCclub; they are separately sold publications. ([Royal Rangers USA, “TRaCclub”](https://royalrangers.com/tracclub))

No first-party source reviewed grants an independent website permission to import the public merit worksheets into its own database or reproduce them verbatim. The public links establish availability, not reuse rights; GCAG’s terms prohibit mirroring or republication without written permission. ([GCAG, “Terms of Use”](https://ag.org/Terms%20of%20Use))

## 3. Trail of the Saber

The official name is **Trail of the Saber**. The national page describes it as recognition for boys who make sustained use of junior servant-leadership development opportunities, beginning in Discovery or Adventure Rangers and continuing through Expedition Rangers. Public display can safely identify the award, its leadership-development purpose, and the official page; detailed qualifications, reading lists, applications, insignia, and saber-use guidance should remain at their official sources. ([Royal Rangers USA, “Trail of the Saber”](https://royalrangers.com/training/junior/tots))

## 4. Gold Medal of Achievement (GMA)

Royal Rangers USA identifies the **Gold Medal of Achievement (GMA)** as its highest award for boys and publishes two further distinctions, **GMA with Merit** and **GMA with Honors**, for continued advancement across additional Program Groups. Ranger Outpost Hub can present those names, their relationship, and an original summary that the award reflects sustained advancement, Bible engagement, active outpost participation, and a capstone service effort. Exact qualifications, applications, and insignia details should remain on the current national page. ([Royal Rangers USA, “Gold Medal of Achievement”](https://royalrangers.com/gma))

## 5. Uniforms

Royal Rangers USA gives each outpost flexibility to select a uniform appropriate to its church and community, and recognizes that choice at local, district, and national events. It lists four formats: ([Royal Rangers USA, “Uniforms”](https://royalrangers.com/uniforms))

- **Utility:** standard/recommended for boys and leaders; displays official insignia.
- **Special:** locally defined custom attire; no Royal Rangers insignia or awards.
- **Dress:** available to adult leaders, Adventure Rangers, and Expedition Rangers.
- **Formal:** for adult leaders only.

The same page links an official Uniform Guide and states that some insignia are restricted. It also says that only items identified as uniform items in national publications or on the national website are authorized. ([Royal Rangers USA, “Uniforms”](https://royalrangers.com/uniforms))

## 6. Pledge, Code, motto, and emblem ideals

The official “About Us” page groups the pledge, an eight-part code, the readiness motto, the Golden Rule, and emblem symbolism under the **Ideals of Royal Rangers**. An original public summary can say that the pledge concerns service, living by the code, and applying the Golden Rule; the code emphasizes alertness, cleanliness, honesty, courage, loyalty, courtesy, obedience, and spirituality; the motto emphasizes readiness; and the emblem’s colors correspond to growth, core church beliefs, and the code themes. The official wording and emblem artwork should remain at the publisher-controlled page. ([Royal Rangers USA, “About Us”](https://royalrangers.com/about); [GCAG, “Terms of Use”](https://ag.org/Terms%20of%20Use))

## 7. Frontiersmen Camping Fellowship (FCF)

FCF is a special Royal Rangers program for men and boys centered on frontier-era outdoor skills, fellowship, personal development, and service. Its advancement path is the **Trail of the Grizzly**, which progresses through three statuses: Frontiersman, Buckskin Frontiersman, and Wilderness Frontiersman. The official page separates the boys’ and adult leaders’ qualifications; Ranger Outpost Hub should link there rather than reproduce them. The page also directs readers to the separately sold **FCF Handbook**, item 020540, for full program detail. ([Royal Rangers USA, “Frontiersmen Camping Fellowship”](https://royalrangers.com/fcf); [GPH, “FCF Handbook”](https://myhealthychurch.com/store/startitem.cfm?item=020540))

### FCF territory/region terminology

A 2023 official national event article used the following USA territory-to-region labels: Trappers–Northwest, Plainsmen–South Central, Mountainmen–Southwest, Voyagers–Great Lakes, Rivermen–Gulf, Colonials–Northeast, Explorers–North Central, and Riflemen–Southeast. ([Royal Rangers USA, “Territorial Rendezvous,” 2023](https://royalrangers.com/en/news/General-News/2023/2023-06-Territorial-Rendezvous?D=%7B39CB0294-58F1-4250-AB82-25E3BF8466D2%7D))

That article was an event announcement, not a current master-data specification. No first-party, current FCF territory/chapter directory or official international FCF-region mapping was located in this review. Whether the 2023 labels remain complete and whether non-U.S. ministries use FCF or the same subdivisions must be confirmed with the national/international rights holder before treating them as authoritative data.

## 9. Rights and licensing constraints

### What the official policies expressly say

1. **Ownership and scope:** GPH states that it owns publications, including curricula and books in print and electronic media, and administers reproduction permissions and logo permissions across media formats. ([GPH, “Rights & Permissions”](https://myhealthychurch.com/store/startcat.cfm?cat=tRIGHTPER))
2. **Narrow quotation allowance:** GPH says permission is not required for fewer than 300 words from a GPH publication for a one-time ministry, educational, or personal use, with no more than 25 text-only photocopies and with a prescribed acknowledgment. All other inquiries require a written or electronic request. ([GPH, “Permission Requests”](https://myhealthychurch.com/store/startcat.cfm?cat=tPERMREQ))
3. **Web reproduction:** GCAG prohibits reproducing, uploading, posting, distributing, mirroring, or reusing its website text or graphics unless the use is expressly permitted or written permission is obtained. It allows links that follow its linking policy. ([GCAG, “Terms of Use”](https://ag.org/Terms%20of%20Use); [GCAG, “Linking Policies”](https://ag.org/Terms-of-Use/Linking-Policies))
4. **GPH site content/scraping:** GPH grants only a personal, limited license to public site areas; it prohibits scraping and, absent express consent, reproduction, republication, upload, public display, distribution, or other exploitation of site content. ([GPH, “Terms of Use”](https://myhealthychurch.com/store/startcat.cfm?cat=tLEGAL))
5. **TRaCclub access and reuse:** TRaCclub memberships belong to named individuals and are non-transferable. The public merit pages provide requirements rather than answers or lesson plans. TRaCclub’s own terms say GPH owns or licenses its materials; only items expressly marked replicable may be reproduced, and the service/materials may not be reproduced, modified, sold, distributed, adapted, or exploited for another purpose without written authorization. ([Royal Rangers USA, “TRaCclub Help Center”](https://royalrangers.com/tracclub/help); [TRaCclub/GPH, “Terms of Use” PDF](https://tracclub.org/Content/files/tc_terms_of_use.pdf))
6. **Curriculum-specific notices:** Official sample curriculum says no part may be reproduced, stored, or transmitted without prior written permission except brief review quotations; its handout exception is limited to teaching within the purchaser’s local outpost. Another official guide says the purchaser may reproduce it for a local outpost/group or self-study. These are limited-use grants, not public-distribution grants. ([GPH, sample Bible merit lesson](https://royalrangers.com/-/media/F4560BF7AB964AF29F0220F6026C0ABB.ashx); [GPH, sample “Bible Doctrines” merit guide](https://royalrangers.com/-/media/C9870D58F197430B81918CF4866C3DD1.ashx))
7. **Marks and artwork:** The Royal Rangers name, group names, logos, emblems, and artwork are registered trademarks or copyrighted properties. Allowed web uses are framed around identifying associated ministry entities or promoting dated ministry events, require unaltered artwork and a prescribed acknowledgment, and direct uncertain cases to AG Rights & Permissions. ([Royal Rangers USA, “Emblems & Trademarks Use”](https://royalrangers.com/policies/trademarks))
8. **Program affiliation:** Churches operating as Royal Rangers agree to use official materials through authorized outlets, follow the acceptable-use policy, renew a charter, and meet the program’s minimum standards. ([Royal Rangers USA, “Program Terms of Use”](https://royalrangers.com/policies/termsofuse))

### Content-by-content status under the published terms

| Content proposed for a website/app | What is established | What remains unresolved |
|---|---|---|
| Full boy handbooks, Leader Manual, or FCF Handbook | They are paid GPH publications in the print formats listed above; the four boys’ handbook pages also advertise e-books. GPH controls electronic reproduction rights. | No reviewed source authorizes hosting, seeding, embedding, or user-uploaded sharing of the full books. A written license would be needed under GPH’s published policy. |
| Complete merit requirement worksheets | The national site deliberately links them for public reading. | The terms do not authorize copying them into another site/database. Permission is needed to reproduce the wording, layouts, compilations, or files; the legal status of independently restating individual factual requirements is not answered by the first-party terms. |
| Merit teaching guides, Bible lessons, leadership lessons, handouts, and award art | These are paid TRaCclub content; membership is individual and non-transferable, and sample files carry local-outpost/self-study limitations. | No public-distribution or platform-rehosting license was located. Whether an item expressly marked “replicable” may be served from a third-party app rather than reproduced locally is not stated. |
| Pledge, Code, motto, award rules, and program descriptions | The official national site publishes them. | No reviewed source expressly licenses continuing verbatim reproduction by an independent app. Exact use should be included in a written permission request. |
| Royal Rangers/age-group/FCF names, emblems, logos, and merit-patch art | They are protected properties with a limited acceptable-use policy. | Whether this particular product is an “associated” ministry entity, what marks it may use, whether it may charge fees, and what notice/approval is required must be resolved in writing. |
| Links to official pages and stores | GCAG’s policy permits links when the source remains clearly identified and no sponsorship is implied. | Framing official pages, obscuring ownership/navigation, or copying the target content is not permitted by that linking policy. |
| Scripture quotations embedded in official materials | Official files identify third-party translations such as NIV and their separate copyright/trademark notices. | Permission from Royal Rangers/GPH does not necessarily clear Bible-translation rights; exact translation and quantity must be checked separately. |

### Permission questions that require written answers

The published sources do not answer these questions for the proposed product:

1. Is the site/app formally authorized to identify itself as a Royal Rangers entity or use Royal Rangers names and marks?
2. Which exact publications and fields may be reproduced: handbook pages, merit titles, requirement wording, award requirements, ideals, FCF material, images, and patch artwork?
3. May approved content be stored in a database, indexed for search, translated, summarized, modified for accessibility, cached offline, or displayed inside native apps?
4. May content be shown only to authenticated users who separately purchased a handbook or TRaCclub membership, and is there an official entitlement/API mechanism?
5. What territories, countries, languages, commercial models, user counts, and term length would a license cover?
6. Who owns and authorizes country-specific/international Royal Rangers content, names, curricula, FCF structures, and translations?
7. Is there an official data feed or update process for age groups, merit requirements, award rules, uniform standards, FCF territories/chapters, and handbook revisions?
8. What exact attribution, trademark symbol, copyright notice, linking behavior, and non-endorsement language is required?
9. Which Scripture translations may be reproduced and under whose license?

GPH’s official Rights & Permissions page asks written requests to specify the organization, material, amount, purpose, and number of copies, and says permission is provided in writing rather than by phone. ([GPH, “Rights & Permissions”](https://myhealthychurch.com/store/startcat.cfm?cat=tRIGHTPER))
